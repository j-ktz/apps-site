How does a pickleball round robin work?

Updated October 11, 2026. The example schedules were generated by Sitout's own rotation engine.

Short answer

In a pickleball round robin, players rotate through a series of doubles games so everyone plays with and against as many people as possible. In the rotating-partners format you get a new partner each round and keep your own individual score. In the fixed-partners format you keep one partner and your team plays every other team.

Four players fit on a court. Anyone extra sits out that round (a bye), and a fair rotation spreads those sit-outs evenly.

Rotating partners is the usual format for open play and league nights. The goal is that you partner as many different people as possible. Sitout spreads partners so repeats are as rare as the math allows, then spreads opponents. An optional mixed-doubles rule keeps teams one man and one woman whenever the players on court allow it; fair sit-outs come first.

Fixed partners works like a classic league table: teams stay together and play each other. Every team plays every other team once before any rematch when you have enough courts.

Individual scoring

In rotating partners, a game's result counts for both players on the team. If Alex and Blake beat Casey and Drew 11–7, Alex and Blake each get a win and +4 point difference, and Casey and Drew each get a loss and −4. Over a night, each player's record mixes games with many partners, so it says more about the player than about one partnership.

Sit-outs (byes)

A court holds four players. With 9 players and 2 courts, 8 play and 1 sits out. A fair rotation picks sit-outs like this:

  1. Whoever has sat out the fewest times sits next.
  2. On a tie, whoever sat out longest ago sits.
  3. If that's still a tie, go by roster order.

That keeps sit-outs within one of each other for every pair of players, and nobody is benched while someone with fewer sit-outs keeps playing. Why not promise that everyone sits once before anyone sits twice? Because it can't always be done: with 12 players on 1 court, 8 sit out each round, so 4 people sit for a second time in round 2.

Late arrivals need one more rule: start them level with the group's fewest sit-outs. Starting them at zero would bench them for several rounds in a row to "catch up" just after they arrived.

How standings are ranked

Sitout ranks players (or teams, in fixed partners) by:

  1. Win rate: wins divided by games played.
  2. Point difference per game.
  3. Total points scored.

Why win rate and not total wins? Fair sit-outs still leave game counts one apart on many nights. With 9 players on 2 courts, after 7 rounds two players have played one more game than the other seven. Ranking on total wins would quietly punish whoever drew the extra sit-out.

How to run a pickleball round robin

  1. Count players and courts

    Four players fit on each court. Everyone beyond that sits out each round, so 9 players on 2 courts means 8 play and 1 sits.

  2. Choose a format

    Rotating partners for open play and mixers, with individual scores. Fixed partners when teams want to stay together.

  3. Set the number of rounds

    With 8 players on 2 courts, 7 rounds gives everyone every partner exactly once. With 9 on 2 courts, 9 rounds does the same and everyone sits out once. For fixed partners with a court for every two teams, play one round fewer than the number of teams, or as many rounds as there are teams when the count is odd.

  4. Choose sit-outs fairly

    Fewest sit-outs sits next. On a tie, whoever sat longest ago, then roster order.

  5. Play and record scores

    Many groups play one game to 11 per round. Each player gets their team's result.

  6. Rank the standings

    Rank by win rate, then point difference per game, then total points.

Questions about round robins

What is a round robin in pickleball?

A format where players rotate through a series of doubles games, so everyone plays with and against many different people instead of staying with one partner against one team. Standings at the end rank everyone by their results.

What's the difference between rotating and fixed partners?

In rotating partners you get a new partner every round and keep an individual score. In fixed partners you keep the same partner all night and your team is ranked against the other teams.

How do byes work in a pickleball round robin?

When there are more players than spots on court (4 per court), the extras sit out that round. A fair rotation has whoever has sat out the fewest times sit next, so sit-outs never differ by more than one between any two players.

How many rounds should a round robin be?

Enough for everyone to partner as many people as possible. With 8 players on 2 courts, 7 rounds gives everyone every partner once. With 9 players on 2 courts, 9 rounds does the same and everyone sits out once.

How do you score a rotating-partner round robin?

Each player gets their team's result for every game they play. Sitout ranks by win rate, then point difference per game, then total points, so a player who sat out one extra round isn't punished for playing one fewer game.

What happens when someone leaves early?

Their scores so far stay in the standings and they simply aren't scheduled again. Only the rounds still to come change, and everyone else's sit-outs stay balanced.

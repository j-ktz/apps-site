How to run pickleball open play

Updated October 12, 2026. Every schedule on this page was generated by Sitout's own rotation engine.

Short answer

Count your players and courts, then run rounds: everyone on court starts together, and the players who sit out are the ones who have sat out least so far. Change partners every round.

Compared with a paddle stack, a set rotation means nobody waits twice while someone else plays three times in a row, and newcomers mix with regulars.

Tips that keep open play moving

  • Use a round timer. Courts finish at different times; a 12 to 15 minute round keeps everyone starting together.
  • Call the next round before the last one ends. Players walk straight to their court.
  • Put the courts on a screen or share an image. Fewer people crowd the organizer's phone.
  • Balance by level if games are lopsided. Spread stronger players across teams so games stay close, without breaking the sit-out rule.

How to run open play with a fair rotation

  1. Count players and courts

    Four players per court. Anyone beyond that sits out this round.

  2. Sit out the fewest-sat first

    Each round, the players with the fewest sit-outs sit. On a tie, whoever sat longest ago sits.

  3. Mix partners every round

    Pair each player with someone they haven't partnered yet tonight.

  4. Start every court together

    Use a timer or a game to 11, then call the next round for everyone at once.

  5. Handle late arrivals and early exits

    Someone who arrives mid-night starts level with the group's fewest sit-outs, so they aren't benched round after round to catch up. Someone who leaves keeps every score they already have.

Questions about running open play

What is paddle stacking in pickleball?

Waiting players put their paddles in a line or rack; when a court frees up, the next four paddles go on. It needs no organizer but can leave some players waiting much longer than others.

How do you rotate players fairly in open play?

Run rounds and sit out the players who have sat out least so far. Sit-outs then never differ by more than one between any two players.

How long should an open play round be?

Games to 11 take about 12 to 15 minutes. A timer of that length keeps every court changing over together.

What do you do when someone arrives late?

Add them to the next round at the group's fewest sit-outs, so they aren't benched round after round to catch up.

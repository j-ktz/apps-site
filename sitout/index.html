Who sits out, round by round

    The rule: whoever has sat out the fewest times sits next. On a tie, whoever sat longest ago sits, then roster order. So sit-outs never differ by more than one between any two players, and nobody is benched while someone with fewer sit-outs keeps playing.     How a night works

    From the first serve to the final standings, on one phone.

    1. Add players and courts

      Type in names. That's all a player needs to be. Pick a format and the number of courts, and turn on the mixed-doubles rule if you want it.

    2. Play the rounds

      Sitout shows each court, who partners whom and who sits out. Enter scores fast between games. Latecomers join and early leavers go mid-night without losing a score, and a wrong tap can be undone.

    3. Share the standings

      End the night with standings for everyone. Share cards for the next round and the final standings are free, and players can see them without the app.

    Why it's fair

    Fairness you can check, not just trust.

    Every rotation follows rules you can read right here. They're the same rules the app runs.

    Sit-outs

    Sit-outs never differ by more than one between any two players. Nobody is benched while someone with fewer sit-outs keeps playing. Ties go to whoever sat longest ago, so sit-outs spread through the night instead of piling up at the end.

    Partners

    Sitout spreads partners so repeats are as rare as the math allows, then spreads opponents. With 8 players on 2 courts, everyone partners everyone else exactly once in 7 rounds. See the rotation.

    Late arrivals, early exits

    Someone who walks in at round 5 starts level with the group's fewest sit-outs, so they aren't benched to catch up. Someone who leaves early keeps every score already entered. Only the rounds still to come change.

    Mixed doubles

    Turn on the mixed-doubles rule and Sitout keeps teams one man and one woman whenever the players on court allow it. Fair sit-outs come first, so nobody sits out of turn just to make a team mixed.

    Formats

    Two ways to play.

    Rotating partners

    A new partner every round, your own score.

    Everyone keeps an individual record: your team's result counts for you, whoever you played with. Add the mixed-doubles rule for a mixer.

    Fixed partners

    Bring a partner, play every team.

    Teams stay together all night and standings are per team. Every team plays every other team once before any rematch when you have enough courts.

    Standings rank by win rate, then point difference per game, then total points. Ranking by win rate means the player who drew one extra sit-out isn't punished for playing one fewer game.

    Questions

    Frequently asked questions

    Do players need the app?

    No. Only the organizer installs Sitout. Players never pay and never need an account. They see the rounds and standings on the organizer's phone, or on the share cards the organizer sends.

    How does Sitout choose who sits out?

    Whoever has sat out the fewest times sits next. On a tie, whoever sat longest ago sits, then roster order. Sit-outs never differ by more than one between any two players, and nobody is benched while someone with fewer sit-outs keeps playing.

    Can people arrive late or leave early?

    Yes. Add a latecomer or mark someone as gone at any point in the night. Scores already entered stay put, and only the rounds still to come change. A latecomer starts level with the group's fewest sit-outs, so they aren't benched to catch up.

    Does Sitout keep mixed doubles mixed?

    With the mixed-doubles rule on, it keeps teams one man and one woman whenever the players on court allow it. Fair sit-outs come first, so on a night with many more men than women (or the other way around), some teams will be two men or two women rather than benching someone out of turn.

    How are standings ranked?

    By win rate, then point difference per game, then total points. Win rate keeps things fair when sit-outs leave some players with one game fewer than others.

    What's free and what's in Sitout Pro?

    Any single night is free, at any size, in either format, with standings and share cards. Sitout Pro adds saved seasons with standings across weeks, group history and DUPR CSV export, for $29.99 a year or $69.99 once for lifetime.

    Does Sitout need an account or the internet?

    It needs no account. Sitout has no server: players, scores and standings are stored on the organizer's iPhone and leave it only when the organizer shares a card or a CSV file.

    Does it work with an odd number of players?

    Yes. Any number works. Four players fit on each court and everyone else sits out that round, chosen by the fair rule above. The guide on how many players you need has a table for 4 to 32 players on 1 to 8 courts.

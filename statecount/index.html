Count your days in every state

If you split your year between places, Statecount keeps a day-by-day record of where you were, and warns you before you reach a day limit you set, like 183 days.

Who it's for

  • Snowbirds who winter in Florida, Arizona or Texas and spend the rest of the year up north.
  • Remote workers who work from different states through the year.
  • Second-home owners who divide their time between two houses.
  • Frequent travelers who keep an eye on days spent in a country.

Why keep a record of your days?

Many states and countries use the number of days you spend there to decide whether you count as a resident for tax purposes. The rules differ from place to place, and you may be asked to show where you were on particular days. Memory and a rough spreadsheet are hard to rely on. A day-by-day record, with where each entry came from, is much easier to stand behind.

Statecount keeps that record for you on your phone, tells you how many days you have left before a limit you set, and never sends your location anywhere.

Statecount doesn't know which rules apply to you. Ask a tax professional about your situation.

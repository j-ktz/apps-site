How to find answers fast in an open-book electrical exam

Tab your code book before exam day, start every lookup from the index, and practice against a clock. Most journeyman electrician exams are open book with a time limit, so the skill that matters is finding the right section quickly, not memorizing it.

1. Check what your exam allows

Rules on tabs, highlighting and notes in the code book vary by state and testing provider. Read your candidate handbook first, so the book you practice with is the book they'll let you bring.

2. Tab the book before exam day

Tab the parts you'll reach for most, so you can open close to the answer and finish with the index. A common starting set:

  • Article 100 (definitions)
  • Articles 210 and 220 (branch circuits and load calculations)
  • Articles 230 and 240 (services and overcurrent protection)
  • Article 250 (grounding and bonding)
  • Articles 300, 310 and 314 (wiring methods, conductors and ampacity tables, boxes)
  • Article 430 (motors) and Article 450 (transformers)
  • Chapter 9 tables and Annex C (conduit and tubing fill)

Use one color per group and keep the tabs readable from the edge. Some candidates add a tab for each table they use in calculations.

3. Use the index, not the table of contents

Pick the most specific word in the question ("receptacle", "bathroom", "ground-fault") and look it up in the index. It points to the section, and your tab gets you to the right part of the book quickly.

4. Practice against a clock

Divide your exam's time limit by its number of questions. That's your average time per question. Practice lookups until most take less than that, and skip anything that's eating your time. You can come back to it at the end.

5. For calculations, write the reference first

Before doing the math, note the section or table you're using. It keeps you from mixing up tables, and it lets you check your work quickly if time allows.

Quick answers

Are journeyman electrician exams open book?

Many are, but not all, and the rules on tabs and notes vary. Check your state's candidate handbook.

What should I tab in the NEC?

Most people tab the articles they use most (definitions, branch circuits and load calculations, services, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods and conductors, motors, transformers) plus the Chapter 9 tables and Annex C.

How do I get faster at looking things up?

Practice with a timer, start from the index with the most specific word in the question, and skip anything that runs long.
